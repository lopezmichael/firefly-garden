Why the main path stops at four minutes

A chapter ends on its hardest level. With five-minute peaks allowed after chapter 10, simulated newcomers (about 1.4 times slower) hit a wall at every peak and none got past chapter 15. Capping the main path at four minutes keeps — of newcomers going through chapter 15. Five-minute levels stay possible as optional challenges.

Most of the work is bookkeeping; overlap and lookahead carry the hard steps

Deduction steps across every census level, by technique, in the order the grader tries them.

Decided on November 12

  • Overlap is now T3, lookahead T4. The first census showed 79 of 142 lookahead levels could be solved with overlapping-bush reasoning instead, because the eight-neighbor rule makes bushes share neighbors constantly. Overlap is the easier, more teachable idea, so it now gets its own tier before lookahead.
  • Mushrooms count their 4 side neighbors. A mushroom is now a numbered blocker that counts only up, down, left and right, unlike a bush, which counts all 8. The experiment below chose it.
  • No level over five minutes. Every shipped level is estimated at — or less, every chapter 1 level at — or less, and every chapter 3 level at — or less. The generator stops hiding clues before a level crosses the limit, and CI fails if a committed level breaks it.
  • Scores separate levels within a tier. The hardest technique still picks a 25-point band (T2 is 50 to 74), and how much searching a level demands now places it inside the band on a log scale, so doubling the effort moves a level the same distance anywhere in the band.

Four kinds of portal player, a thousand of each, play the chapters above step by step along each level's grader trace. Steps at their skill take normal time; one tier above is a stretch that can get them stuck; solving stretch steps teaches the technique. Stuck players buy a hint, watch an ad for one, struggle on, or quit, and some who quit leave for good. Every rate here comes from that model, not from real players.

Hint economy: generosity decides how many newcomers stay

Eighteen economies run through the same simulation. The price of a hint barely changes how often players want one; it changes who pays. When a stuck newcomer cannot afford a hint and skips the ad, they usually quit.

What this means

Squeezing players toward hint ads loses ad views overall. A tight economy adds a fraction of a rewarded ad per player but keeps fewer of them playing, so they see fewer midgame ads. Slower players see the most midgame ads because they play longest; experts see the fewest. Keeping people in the game is the revenue lever, so the generous economy stays.

Next-level policy: a short detour when a player needs one

After each play the game picks what comes next. By default it is the next level on the main path. It inserts one detour from a reserve of extra levels (never main-path levels) when recent play suggests the player needs it, and never two detours in a row.

  • Back after a break (8 hours or more): a quick warm-up under a minute, one tier below the hardest technique they have used without help.
  • Quit a level: an easier breather, then the level they quit comes back.
  • A long solve (well past the estimate, or over 4 minutes): an easier breather.
  • Hints on two levels running: an easier breather.

The simulation plays sittings of 8 to 20 minutes, 12 hours apart, through the same next-level function the game will use. It cannot know how much a breather soothes a real player, so it runs twice: once with no effect on quitting, and once assuming 30% fewer players leave after a quit when an easier level comes next. That assumption is a placeholder until Phase 3 measures it.

How levels are graded
T1 bookkeeping
A bush already has its number, so its other neighbors stay empty; or it needs every open neighbor, so fill them.
T2 one source
A dark tile that only one open cell can light.
T2 bush room
A bush needs k more fireflies and only some open spots can hold k at once without seeing each other.
T3 overlap
Two bushes share open neighbors; together their numbers pin the shared and unshared cells.
T4 lookahead
Assume a cell, apply the moves that become obvious, and hit a contradiction.
Hints
A hint places one correct firefly or fixes one wrong one, taken from the grader's next step. A rewarded ad grants 3 hints: one used now, two saved.
Simulated players
Newcomer: knows T1, 1.4x slower, 150 s patience. Casual: knows T2, 1.15x, 240 s. Puzzler: knows T3, 0.9x, 420 s. Expert: knows T4, 0.7x, 600 s. A stretch step (one tier above) takes 2.5x as long and gets a player stuck 25% of the time (50% when it is the only move); two tiers above, 85%. Learning the next tier takes 2 to 3 unaided stretch steps.
Mushroom
A numbered blocker that counts fireflies on its 4 side tiles only.
T5 deep trial
Assume a cell and follow T1 to T3 moves many steps until a contradiction.
Score
The hardest technique picks a 25-point band (T1 25 to 49, T2 50 to 74, and so on). Search effort (the time estimate without taps) places the level inside its band on a log scale, from that tier's easiest to its hardest levels in the census.
Estimated time
Seconds per step by tier (2.5, 4, 12, 18, 40), slower when fewer moves are available, plus 0.8 s per firefly tapped. A model to calibrate against playtests.
