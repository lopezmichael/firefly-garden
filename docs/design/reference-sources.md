# Where to look for references

A hunting list for the character exploration (`docs/plans/2026-11-12-character-exploration.md`).
Michael curates a short list from this; the moodboards are built from his list plus public-domain
finds. References are for **feel only**: nothing from a game, board game or living artist goes
into the build. Each entry gives a place to look and what to look for there.

For each thing you send, a few words help: *palette*, *texture*, *light*, *creatures*,
*plants*, *UI*, *layout*, or *"not this"*.

## 1. Board games (new)

Board games have solved a problem we share: a small, tactile, top-down play surface that has to
read at a glance and still feel like a place. Look at the box art, and also at the **tiles,
cards, tokens and player boards**, which are the closest thing to our board and chrome.
BoardGameGeek has a user photo gallery for every game (search the title, then open *Images*):
https://boardgamegeek.com

Art in Board Games, an interview series with board game illustrators about process:
https://www.moregamesplease.com/art-in-boardgames

### Painted and watercolour (direction 1)

| Game | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| Wingspan (and Wyrmspan) | Watercolour bird cards: soft washes on white, crisp edges where it matters |
| Floriferous, Herbaceous, Sunset Over Water (Pencil First Games) | Watercolour flowers and gardens on cards; the closest match to our subject |
| Meadow | Botanical and insect cards, naturalist-plate feel |
| Arboretum, Calico | Gentle painted textures on cards and tiles |
| Kanagawa | Ink-wash painting as the theme; brush edges, paper |
| Mysterium, Dixit | Dreamlike painted cards; storybook mood (heavier than we want) |
| Tokaido | Flat, calm, generous negative space |

### Layered, stand-up and diorama (direction 2)

| Game | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| Photosynthesis | Cardboard trees standing on the board: a real diorama, shadows as a mechanic |
| Everdell | The 3D Ever Tree and the woodland tableau as a place |
| Takenoko | Stacked bamboo and garden plots: a garden built on the table |
| Harmonies | Layered tokens building a landscape; very tactile |
| Cascadia, Azul, Patchwork | Tile feel: thickness, edges, how pieces sit on a surface |

### Printmaking, flat inks, lanterns (direction 3)

| Game | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| Parks (and Trails) | Art from the Fifty-Nine Parks screenprint series: limited inks, bold shapes, print grain |

## 2. Video games (from the brief, with what to look for)

Game UI Database (search a title to see its menus and HUD): https://www.gameuidatabase.com and
Interface In Game: https://interfaceingame.com

- **Watercolour:** Alba, Old Man's Journey, Florence, Botany Manor, Cozy Grove, Gris, Spiritfarer.
  How they keep text and buttons readable on painted surfaces.
- **Papercraft:** Tearaway, Paper Mario: The Origami King, Lumino City (built by hand from real
  card), Paper Cut Mansion (pencil on real paper, menus too), Kirby's Epic Yarn, and Monument
  Valley for diorama framing.
- **Woodcut and ink:** Okami, Pentiment, Inkle's 80 Days and Heaven's Vault, The Banner Saga,
  Darkest Dungeon (ink weight).
- **Cozy puzzle benchmarks:** Meowdoku, A Short Hike, Unpacking, Islanders, Townscaper.

## 3. Illustration and picture books

### Public domain (usable as reference, sometimes as source)

- **Arthur Rackham, Edmund Dulac, Kay Nielsen, Beatrix Potter, Ida Rentoul Outhwaite**:
  watercolour and ink, fairies, gardens, small creatures. Old Book Illustrations:
  https://www.oldbookillustrations.com
- **Hiroshige and Hokusai**: night scenes, fireflies, lanterns, gardens. Search *Hiroshige
  fireflies* and *firefly catching* (Eishi and Utamaro have prints of it):
  - The Met, Open Access filter: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection
  - Rijksmuseum: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/rijksstudio
- **Lotte Reiniger**: 1920s cut-paper silhouette films. The whole papercraft idea, backlit.
- **Botanical and insect plates** (bushes, roses, fruit trees, koi, fireflies; search
  *Lampyridae* for fireflies): Biodiversity Heritage Library https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org
- Also: The Public Domain Review https://publicdomainreview.org, Smithsonian Open Access
  https://www.si.edu/openaccess, Library of Congress https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use

### Contemporary (reference only, never copied)

- **Watercolour and storybook:** Jon Klassen, Shaun Tan, Isabelle Arsenault, Elsa Beskow (older,
  public-domain status varies)
- **Papercraft and lit paper:** Hari & Deepti (backlit cut-paper dioramas, the "lit from
  inside" lantern look exactly), Helen Musselwhite (woodland paper sculpture), Su Blackwell (book
  sculptures), Eric Carle (painted tissue collage), Robert Sabuda (pop-up books)
- **Linocut and woodblock:** Angie Lewin and Mark Hearld (British botanical linocut and collage,
  very close to a garden subject), Christopher Brown, Kawase Hasui and Ohara Koson (shin-hanga:
  night, lanterns, birds, flowers; check public-domain status per country)
